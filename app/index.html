How the numbers work

The core idea

Max CPC = (Price − cost) × (1 − buffer) × conversion rate
Target ROAS = Price ÷ [(Price − cost) × (1 − buffer)]

Cost is COGS, plus shipping, other costs, and fees when those cells are filled in. Conversion rate is sales ÷ clicks. Under 50 clicks the row is tagged low confidence. Confidence uses a Wilson lower bound and is a warning, not a different bid.

Increase bid: CPC is under 85% of max. Maintain: 85–105%. Decrease — losing profit: CPC is over max. Unprofitable: cost is already above price. Hold — low stock: stock on hand is below this period’s sales, and the row is not already losing money.

Buffer %

The share of unit profit you refuse to spend on ads. 20% means you will spend at most 80% of unit profit to win the sale. The default at the top applies when a row’s own buffer is empty. Typing 0 keeps 0% — it does not silently become 20%.

On import, 20 and 20% are 20. 1 and 1% are 1, not 100. A value strictly between 0 and 1, such as 0.2, is treated as a fraction and becomes 20. That is how some spreadsheets store a percent.

Spend with zero sales

If ad spend is above zero and sales are 0, the row is “Decrease — losing money.” Net profit is minus the spend. A zero is kept. It is not treated as missing, and it is not labeled “Need data.”

Import

Drop a Shopify product export, a Shopify orders export, a Google Ads shopping or product report, a cost sheet, or an Amazon fee preview or settlement. The header row is detected even when title rows sit above it. Google’s Total row is skipped. You get a mapping preview and can change the file type or any column before import. A file with no recognizable header, or a header and no data rows, is reported as an error. It is not imported as blank products.

Orders are totaled by SKU. Settlement fee lines become fees per unit; tax lines are ignored. Profit uses units from orders when that file is present, and otherwise falls back to ad conversions.

Profit Leak Finder

Profit per unit = price − product cost − fees − (ad spend ÷ units), when units are above zero. Product cost is unit cost + shipping + other. Fees come from an Amazon file when you add one; without it, profit is before marketplace fees and the table says so.

Cut ads: the SKU makes money before ads and loses money after them, or it has spend and zero units. Raise price: cost is over price, and a higher price would clear about a 15% margin. Stop selling: a 25% price increase still would not. Review fees: fees are at least 35% of price and the SKU loses money before ads. Annual leak adds up only the losses, scaled from the period in the file (or one month if no dates were found).

It does not put an arrow on each row. There is one snapshot slot. There are no profiles. Saving is part of Pro and Agency. The free plan keeps the list until you close the tab.

What this can’t tell you

CPC and conversion rate move when you change bids. Max CPC describes the current numbers. It does not predict the point where more spend stops paying. The spend-level tests in Advanced are the only curve this tool will draw, and only between points you actually ran.
